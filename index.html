Ransomware Ready

Learn how ransomware can affect your work, spot common warning signs, and practise what to do when something seems wrong.

1. What is ransomware?

Ransomware is malicious software that can lock you out of files or systems, often by encrypting data. The attacker then demands money to restore access or threatens to expose stolen information.

Remember: Paying a ransom does not guarantee that your files will be restored or that stolen information will be deleted.

How it may arrive

Phishing emails, unsafe attachments, malicious links, compromised websites, or unpatched software.

What you may notice

Files suddenly will not open, unusual file extensions appear, a ransom note appears, or your device behaves unexpectedly.

Why it matters

It can disrupt work, expose sensitive information, cause financial loss, and affect customers or colleagues.

2. Warning signs to watch for

  • An unexpected email urges you to act immediately or threatens account closure.
  • The sender's address or website spelling looks slightly unusual.
  • An attachment or link is unexpected, even if the message appears to come from someone you know.
  • A message asks you to enable macros, install software, or share your password.
  • Your computer displays unfamiliar pop-ups, renamed files, or a ransom demand.

Attackers can imitate trusted people and organisations. Verify unusual requests using a separate, trusted contact method.

3. What should you do?

Before an incident

Use approved security tools, install updates, use unique passwords and multi-factor authentication, and keep work files in approved backed-up locations.

If you suspect an attack

Stop interacting with the message or device. Follow your organisation's incident procedure and contact IT/security immediately using a known channel.

What not to do

Do not click further, forward suspicious files, delete evidence, attempt your own cleanup, or pay the ransom unless your organisation's authorised response team directs you.

Key action: Report quickly—even if you only clicked a link and nothing obvious happened. Early reporting can limit damage.
